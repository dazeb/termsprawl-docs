Termsprawl Cloud is an optional account layer, in preview: encrypted backup of
one project's canvas state, and — on the Canvas plan — your own hosted canvas
space that runs in a browser. This page explains what's backed up, how the
service stores it, how spaces work, and how to enable it all.

The local app needs no account. Everything on this page is opt-in.

## Sign in

Sign in with GitHub — it's a device flow, so no password is typed into
termsprawl.

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Find the Cloud section and click **sign in**.
3. termsprawl shows a device code and opens GitHub for you to approve it.
4. Once approved, you're signed in. The panel shows your GitHub login and plan.

## What an ordinary backup covers

Ordinary Cloud backup captures **one active project's canvas state** — the
selected project's nodes and their layout, uploaded by the signed-in desktop
app.

It does not capture every project, the app's settings, tmux configuration,
your source files, or your shell history. Those stay on your machine.

<Callout type="info" title="Three different things, easily confused">
  - **Ordinary backup** — one active project's canvas state, stored per
    account (this page, above).
  - **Workspace bundle** — every project plus saved terminal scrollback as a
    single JSON file you can export and re-import (Settings → Cloud & backup,
    **export workspace…** / **open workspace…**). It works signed in or not.
  - **Canvas snapshot sync** — the active project's canvas pushed to your
    hosted space, and the space's latest snapshot pulled back into a new
    local project.
</Callout>

## Encryption and storage

Backups travel over **HTTPS**. Stored backups are encrypted **at rest** with
**AES-256-GCM**, using a per-user key derived from the service's
**master key** — one key per account, not one global key. When you open a
backup, the service decrypts it for that authenticated request.

That means the protection is about what sits on disk: it keeps stored data
encrypted and unreachable without an authenticated account. It is not
client-side encryption — the service holds the key material and can read what
it stores. There is no user-held key and nothing here is end-to-end encrypted.

## Back up now

**Settings → Cloud & backup → back up now** uploads a fresh snapshot
immediately. There's no scheduled automatic backup yet — backups happen when
you ask for one.

The dashboard's **Back up now** is a request, not a backup: it asks your
signed-in desktop app to run the backup, and the app completes it the next
time it polls. If the app isn't running, the request waits.

## Plans and billing

Cloud accounts have three tiers:

- **Free** — sign in, and that's it.
- **Pro** — encrypted cloud backup and sync ($2/mo).
- **Canvas** — everything in Pro **plus your own online canvas space** ($5/mo).

Billing is wired but in **test mode**: the prices above are preview prices
while the service is in preview, and **no real card is charged** today. The
status line in Settings shows which plan you're on.

## Cancellation, deletion, and your data

- **Cancelling** stops new backups from being created. The backups you already
  have stay listed and downloadable — the service deliberately keeps read
  access open.
- **Deleting your account** removes the account's cloud backups and the
  space's stored snapshot.
- There is **no per-backup delete action** in the app or dashboard today. To
  remove everything at once, delete your account.

## Online canvas spaces

The **Canvas** plan gives you a hosted termsprawl canvas that runs in your
browser — same nodes, and the space's terminals run in the space itself.
Spaces are live today: one per user, provisioned under your GitHub login.

1. In Settings, under the cloud section, choose **open your online canvas**.
   termsprawl provisions a private space for you (first time takes under a
   minute).
2. Your canvas opens at `canvas.termsprawl.com/<your-login>` in the browser.
3. Work there like the desktop app — terminals, sticky notes, agents, and
   projects all work, and the layout is saved as you go.

### Keeping desktop and online canvas in sync

Settings shows the space rows for the signed-in user:

- **Open your online canvas** — provisions (or reopens) the space and signs
  you in with a short-lived token.
- **Open online snapshot** — pulls the space's latest canvas into a NEW local
  project, so you can look at (or fork) the online state without touching
  your current work.
- **Sync this project online** — pushes the active project's canvas to your
  space.

<Callout type="info" title="Snapshot sync, not live merge">
  Sync is pull-model snapshots: the online canvas stores the latest snapshot
  of your canvas, and the desktop pushes or pulls whole snapshots. Your
  terminals' tmux sessions stay on the machine they run on — the online
  canvas runs its own terminals in the hosted space.
</Callout>

### Spaces lifecycle

- **Stop** — the space pauses (no resource use); your canvas is kept. The
  dashboard's SpaceCard has a confirm-gated Stop button. Stop is deliberate:
  the space stays stopped until you press **Resume**, so an old browser tab or
  bookmark can't quietly start it again.
- **Idle spaces stop themselves** — a space nobody touches for 30 minutes is
  stopped automatically; opening it again wakes it (takes a few seconds) with
  your canvas intact.
- **Deleting your account** removes the space's stored snapshot (see above).

### Security and isolation

Each hosted space runs in its **own isolated container network** — no two
spaces share a network. Cloud API routes that hold your data (backups,
sessions, sync, space snapshots) are scoped to your account, carry `no-store`
caching headers, and a two-account regression suite checks that one account
cannot read another's data. Your backups and snapshots are encrypted at rest;
the sync token a space uses to push snapshots expires after 30 days and is
re-minted on every container start.

## What's next

The hosted single canvas space is in preview and running today. Broader
managed-platform work — the browser edition as a managed service, and wider
remote access such as SSH relay and operations — is in development on the
app's core, and **no date is promised** for it. When it ships, it will be
documented here as shipped, not before.

<Callout type="tip" title="The local app stands alone">
  Closed apps lose nothing because your sessions live in tmux. Cloud is the
  optional next step: move to a new machine, sign in, and open your hosted
  canvas — with the account's backups there when you need them.
</Callout>
